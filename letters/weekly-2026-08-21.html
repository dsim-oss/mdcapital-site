01

Macro Environment

The Fed didn't do anything this period. The Treasury did, and it turned out to matter more. On August 19 Secretary Bessent doubled the maximum size of the Treasury's long-end buyback operations, from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, covering the 10-to-20-year and 20-to-30-year sectors and running from September 9 through the November 4 refunding. The stated reason was liquidity support. The context is that long-dated yields had climbed to levels last seen roughly two decades ago, and repurchasing debt puts cash back into the market and pushes long yields down. Risk assets read it the way risk assets read any liquidity injection, and Bitcoin read it loudest.

Underneath that, the September rate picture inverted. The July employment report came in badly: payrolls fell by 23,000 against an expected gain of about 85,000, with wage growth at 3.2% trailing inflation at 3.4%. Odds of a September hike dropped from 67% at the end of July to roughly 44% by August 7, and have since fallen further to about 29% as of August 20. Chair Warsh now sits between a job market that's cracking and inflation that has run above target for 64 consecutive months. Add a Treasury actively working to suppress the long end and you have a Fed being crowded by its own government's debt manager. That tension is the macro story into the fall.

On the political side, the President spent the week pressing Congress on the CLARITY Act and met with crypto executives. It helped sentiment. I'd hold the enthusiasm loosely: Galaxy's Alex Thorn cut his odds of that bill becoming law in 2026 to roughly 10% in mid-August, down from 60% in May, and prediction markets sit near 17%. Rallies built on legislation that hasn't passed tend to give back what the liquidity didn't earn.

02

Bitcoin This Period

Ten quiet days, then four loud ones. From the August 7 close at $64,745, Bitcoin drifted sideways and slightly lower into mid-month while spot ETFs shed a net $390 million over four straight days of outflows, the heaviest weekly withdrawal in six weeks. By August 17 it sat just above $63,000 with the Fear and Greed index at 38 and 30-day implied volatility near its lows for the year. The market had no catalyst and was pricing accordingly.

Then the Treasury announcement landed on the 19th and the flows turned on a dime. Spot ETFs took in $517 million on August 19 alone. Bitcoin cleared $67,178, took out its early-June high near $69,994 overnight and closed the 20th around $72,000, then ran through $75,000 in Asian hours on the 21st and touched $79,400 before settling near $77,379, up roughly 23% on the week and 7% on the day. That's the strongest week Bitcoin has had since March 2023. Sentiment followed price the way it always does: 38 to 71 in four sessions, from Fear to Greed, and it now sits at the highest reading since the spring.

Two things about that run are worth marking. The first is that Bitcoin is now 34% above the June low of $57,650 and only 12% below our blended purchase cost, which was 26% two weeks ago. The second is that the underlying gauges disagree with the headline. Whale activity and order-book pressure both sit in extreme fear territory even as price impulse and search interest print in the nineties. That's a rally led by retail flow and index buying with the large holders sitting on their hands. It can continue for a while. It isn't the same thing as the accumulation base that formed in June.

03

Featured: The Issuer Changes How It Defends Par

Correction to the last letter, issued August 9 I wrote two weeks ago that STRC's dividend "steps up 0.5% whenever STRC trades below $95" and that "the mechanism only moves one way." That description is no longer accurate and I'm correcting it in the same place I published it. Strategy has moved off the automatic escalator. Management now says it will "not necessarily raise the dividend solely because STRC trades below its stated amount," and sets the rate against market price, credit spreads, competing yields, Bitcoin volatility, and cash-reserve coverage. August was held at 12% rather than ratcheted to 12.5%. The seven prior increases were real; the guarantee I implied around future ones was not.

What replaced the ratchet is more interesting than the ratchet was. Between August 10 and August 16, Strategy sold 3,458,866 of its own common shares for $333.7 million and bought no Bitcoin with the proceeds. The money went three places: $52.4 million to STRC dividends, $132.2 million to repurchasing STRC, and $149.1 million into reserves, which now stand at $4.8 billion. Two and a half dollars of buyback for every dollar of coupon, on the same security, in the same week. The effect showed up immediately. STRC opened at $95.39 on August 12 for a two-month high and closed this week at $96.18, back above the $95 line that used to trigger the escalator.

Read the arithmetic behind that choice and the logic is plain. Every 50 basis points of rate increase costs Strategy about $52.3 million a year, permanently, across roughly $10.46 billion of outstanding stated value. Buying the stock back is cheaper than raising the coupon, it shrinks the share count that the coupon is paid on, and it lifts the price above the level where the pressure to raise comes from. The company has swapped a permanent obligation for a one-time cash outlay. For a holder of the instrument, the par ceiling is unchanged and the floor is now defended with capital rather than with a promise.

Our par-ceiling thesis survives this intact, and in one respect it gets stronger. An issuer buying below par to protect the price puts its own bid underneath ours, which makes sub-par accumulation the company's policy as well as our doctrine. The catch is where the capital comes from. Strategy now operates a Digital Credit Capital Framework with three authorizations: up to $1.25 billion of Bitcoin monetization to fund its USD reserve, up to $1 billion to repurchase its preferred series, and up to $1 billion of common buyback. It sold 1,690 BTC in the week to August 10, taking holdings from 842,137 to 840,447. The company that made never selling its Bitcoin the entire premise of its existence is now selling Bitcoin, in part, to defend the price of the preferred we own. I want that stated in a letter rather than buried in a risk footnote, because it is the clearest signal yet that the income leg and the growth leg of this fund are not independent bets. They sit on the same balance sheet, and that balance sheet has started making choices between them.

A hold confirms the buyback has replaced the escalator. A raise anyway would tell us the price defense is costing more than the coupon, which would be the more troubling read.

Hike odds have fallen to roughly 29% as of August 20, down from 67% at the end of July, on a jobs print that turned negative. A hold into a long end the Treasury is actively easing eases both of the pressures that have capped this asset all year, which are the threat of tighter policy and a rising cost of money at the long end. A hawkish surprise against a market now sitting at 71 on the sentiment gauge is the setup for a sharp give-back, and a give-back is what re-opens the accumulation window.
  • Metric #11 is closer to firing than it looks. Tolerance utilization sits at 30% and rises with every point Bitcoin outruns the income leg. On today's marks it crosses 50% at a Bitcoin price of $82,339, only 6.4% above spot, and 80% at $90,995. At 50% the response is already written: dividends stop reinvesting into STRC and start accumulating toward Swan transfers.   • The keystone needs a source of growth. The Bitcoin count doesn't move on its own. Risks We Are Watching

  • The issuer is selling Bitcoin to defend our income asset. Strategy's new framework authorizes up to $1.25 billion of Bitcoin monetization plus $2 billion of combined preferred and common buybacks, it sold 1,690 BTC in the week to August 10, and it spent $132.2 million buying back STRC in a single week. Our two largest sleeves outside spot depend on that same balance sheet. If Bitcoin revisits the lows, the company faces a choice between selling more Bitcoin and letting the preferred slip, and both outcomes hit us. This remains the fund's single largest concentration of issuer risk and it grew this period.
  • The blend lags in up-tapes, and this is what that looks like. A 23% week in Bitcoin against a 1% week in STRC moved the full-cycle audit seven points in fourteen days. If Bitcoin runs from here, that number gets worse before the coupon earns it back. The buffer is 12% a year, the drag is consuming 30% of it, and the response is defined at 50% rather than improvised.
  • A flat keystone in a rising market. The Bitcoin count is the metric I chose to be judged on, and it printed zero growth while the dollar book rose 11%. A quarter of that is a bad quarter regardless of what NAV does. The honest constraint is that the fund is fully invested with 2.1% cash and cannot stack without a decision or a contribution.
  • Sentiment at 71 with whales in extreme fear. The rally's internals are thinner than its price. Retail flow and index buying moved this tape while large holders sat out. That combination has a history of retracing quickly, and the fund has almost no dry powder to answer it with.
  • Derived cash figures. IBKR cash of $53 and bank cash of $472 both carry forward from the ledger and the prior reconciled balance. Neither has been trued against a statement since the last letter and neither materially moves NAV.

Stepping back: the market handed us 11% of NAV for doing nothing, and the scoreboard we actually keep says we stood still. Both of those are worth saying out loud in the same paragraph, because the temptation in a week like this one is to report the first and quietly drop the second. The drawdown against your capital narrowed from 15.2% to 5.7% and the fund is now 10.7% of a Bitcoin move away from whole, against 33% two weeks ago and 46% in May. That's real progress on the dollar scoreboard. On the Bitcoin scoreboard the count sits exactly where June left it, and the blend has given back its first-ever lead in the full-cycle audit. Dollars are how we settle; Bitcoin is how we keep score. The next letter is due in September and will carry the STRC rate decision, the FOMC outcome, and whatever the tape does with a market that just went from fear to greed in four days.
