Macro Environment

The macro story of the quarter was a Federal Reserve that refused to blink. The June meeting delivered the most hawkish hold in years and sank risk assets into month-end. The July 28–29 meeting held the line again, 9 votes to 3, with Chair Warsh stripping forward guidance from the statement entirely and three regional presidents dissenting in favor of a hike. Futures now price a better-than-even chance of a 25 basis point increase in September. Layer on a Middle East conflict that spent June escalating before de-escalation signals emerged in July, and you have the regime Bitcoin has traded in all year: down roughly 45% year over year, with rallies sold into every hawkish data point.

Within crypto, the flow picture told the whole story. Spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $4.5 billion in June, record outflows with IBIT leading, and that selling did what selling does to a market with no marginal bid. Then in early July the flows reversed: the first five-day inflow streak since April, followed by a three-week run of net inflows as traders unwound their downside hedges. Price followed flows in both directions, which stays the cleanest read on this market. Watch the flows, not the forecasts.

03

Featured: The Par Defense, Stress-Tested

In May we wrote that STRC is engineered to trade at par: capped above $100 by Strategy's at-the-market issuance, defended below it by a dividend ratchet. This quarter the market ran a live stress test on the second half of that claim, and the results are worth banking.

As Bitcoin capitulated, STRC broke to depths nobody had seen. Through the $95 de-anchor line in mid-June, to $89 by June 18, and eventually printing as low as $71.25 intraquarter, nearly 29% below par. Below par, Strategy's ATM goes quiet, so the whole defense fell to the ratchet: the dividend steps up 0.5% for any month the stock trades below $95, and the step-ups are permanent. Strategy has now raised the rate seven straight times, from 9% at launch to 12% for August, confirmed by Saylor on August 1. The market keeps testing whether an issuer will honor an open-ended, one-way yield escalator. So far the answer has been yes, every month, without exception.

Be clear-eyed about what this means. The bear case, made openly by analysts, is that this is “a capital structure that survives volatility only by adding permanent obligations.” Each ratchet raises the perpetual cost of the whole preferred stack, and Q2 forced Strategy to sell 1,638 BTC and issue equity to keep servicing it. That risk is real, and it lives in Section 07. For a holder whose entire position sits below par, though (our lifetime average fill is now $93.73), the stress test has been generous: every month the defense holds, the coupon on our sub-par dollars gets richer. The 39.7 shares we bought at an average of $85.00 carry a yield-on-cost above 14% at the new rate, locked in for as long as we hold them and the dividend gets paid. Sub-par accumulation was always the only meaningful entry into this instrument. This quarter the market offered the deepest sub-par window in STRC's existence, and we took it with both hands.

The DRIP Decision — A Documented Reversal

The May letter planned to switch off STRC dividend reinvestment after the May 30 distribution. We didn't do it, and the omission has been ratified into a decision: the DRIP stays on. The original rationale was sound at the time. With STRC pressing $100, each reinvested dividend bought shares at the instrument's capital ceiling with nothing left to appreciate. The June break changed the input. Reinvestments have since filled at $98.10, $85.95, $87.68, and $90.03, and a dividend reinvested fifteen points below par is sub-par accumulation on autopilot, twice a month, at yields-on-cost above 13%. The policy, then and now, is sub-par accumulation; the toggle just serves it. When STRC returns to pressing par, the DRIP goes off and dividends accumulate toward Swan transfers, as originally planned. Until then the autopilot is doing doctrine's work and we leave it alone.

The benchmark is BTC, not dollars. Document before execute. Size to the signal, not the headline. No leverage on spot BTC, ever.

On that last line: the one instrument in our book that runs leverage on Bitcoin, Strategy's common at 2% of NAV, fell 43% this quarter. Our unleveraged spot stack fell 19% with the asset and then grew 41.5% in units, because we could buy the low instead of servicing a margin call at it. That's what the leverage rule protects.

In a regime where the Fed is debating hikes, patience is the position, and the income leg pays us roughly 5.4% of NAV a year to hold it.
